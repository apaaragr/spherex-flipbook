About this data

How it works

  1. Find every image. NASA's SPHEREx telescope maps the whole sky every six months in 102 infrared colours. We list every SPHEREx image that covers the chosen point (NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive).
  2. Cut out and clean. We download small cutouts, subtract the zodiacal-light glow from solar-system dust, and blank out pixels the mission flags as bad (cosmic rays, dead or hot pixels, persistence). Saturated star cores are kept for display and marked.
  3. Align. SPHEREx looks at the sky at a different rotation each time, so every cutout is re-projected onto one north-up grid with SPHEREx's native 6.15″ pixels.
  4. Stack each pass. The median of all images from one six-month pass gives one clean frame per detector; the "All" frame combines the six detectors for extra depth.
  5. Check against a catalogue. We compare the star's measured shift with the motion predicted by Gaia/CatWISE, and use nearby non-moving stars as a noise floor, so you can see the motion is real.

This publication makes use of data products from the Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization and Ices Explorer (SPHEREx), a joint project of the Jet Propulsion Laboratory and the California Institute of Technology, funded by NASA. Data served by the NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive (IRSA). Reference positions and motions from ESA Gaia DR3 and CatWISE2020. Design inspired by WiseView.

Use of AI: code, analysis and text for this project were developed with help from Claude (Anthropic), an AI assistant. All images are real SPHEREx data; all results were checked against independent catalogues.
