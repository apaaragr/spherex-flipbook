Catch the sky in motion.

NASA's SPHEREx telescope photographs the entire sky every six months in 102 infrared colours. SPHEREx Flipbook lines those photos up so anyone can spot stars and brown dwarfs on the move: the same trick that found Pluto.

How to find a moving star

1

Line up the photos

We fetch every SPHEREx image of a patch of sky, clean out the solar-system dust glow and bad pixels, and align them onto one north-up grid.

2

Blink them

Each six-month pass becomes one frame. Flip between frames, or subtract them: anything that moved shows up as a red–blue pair.

3

Check it's real

We compare the measured motion with the Gaia and CatWISE catalogues, using nearby steady stars as the noise floor.

This publication makes use of data products from the Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization and Ices Explorer (SPHEREx), a joint project of the Jet Propulsion Laboratory and the California Institute of Technology, funded by NASA. Data served by the NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive (IRSA). Reference positions and motions from ESA Gaia DR3 and CatWISE2020. Viewer design inspired by WiseView.

Use of AI: code, analysis and text for this project were developed with help from Claude (Anthropic), an AI assistant. All images are real SPHEREx data; all results were checked against independent catalogues.
