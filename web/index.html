NASA Space Apps 2026 · Planet X and SPHEREx

Catch the sky in motion.

NASA's SPHEREx telescope photographs the whole sky every six months. We line those photos up so anyone can watch nearby stars move: the same trick that found Pluto.

You are here: one star among a few hundred billion, about 26,000 light-years from the centre of a galaxy 100,000 light-years across.

    How it works

    From raw NASA images to a moving star

    1

    Line up the photos

    We fetch every SPHEREx image of a patch of sky, clean out the glow of solar-system dust and the bad pixels, and align them onto one north-up grid.

    2

    Blink them

    Each six-month pass becomes one frame. Flip between frames, or subtract them: anything that moved shows up as a red–blue pair.

    3

    Check it's real

    We compare the measured motion with the Gaia and CatWISE catalogues, using nearby steady stars as the noise floor, and say so when the data can't decide.
